A church does not run on one tool. It runs on a website, a member database, online giving, kids' check-in, a place for people to talk to each other, courses, a newsletter and tax receipts at the end of the year. That is the real list, and no church picked it — the list picked them.

Harvest sells one subscription that covers that list. Tithely sells church software as a catalogue of separate products, each with its own price on their own pricing page — Church Management at $72/mo, a Custom Church App at $89/mo, a Custom Church Website at $19/mo, Worship Planning at $29/mo, Text-to-Give at $19/mo, with Free Giving at $0/mo on top of card processing ([get.tithe.ly/pricing](https://get.tithe.ly/pricing), read 1 September 2026). That is the whole comparison, and everything below is just arithmetic on it.

## Build the stack and read the total

Take a church that wants the ordinary things: a site, a database, giving, check-in, an app their congregation can install, a way to plan Sunday and schedule volunteers, and somewhere to put a course.

Four of those come straight off Tithely's own page — Church Management, the app, the website and Worship Planning: $72 + $89 + $19 + $29, or **$209 a month** à la carte, before a single donation is processed and before the course platform, which is not on that page at all. Tithely also sells those four products, plus Text-to-Give, bundled as All Access at **$119/mo**, listed against a struck-through $228 (same page, read 1 September 2026). Breeze ChMS, which many churches still search for by that name, is the same product: their pricing page now carries a banner saying Breeze has been rebranded as Tithely Church Management, at $72/mo ([breezechms.com/pricing](https://www.breezechms.com/pricing), read 1 September 2026).

Harvest's Ministry plan is **$63.34/mo, billed as $760 every 12 months** — one plan, one bill, one login. Against the à la carte stack that is a difference of $145.66 a month. Against Tithely's own bundle it is still $55.66 a month, roughly $668 a year, which is a summer camp scholarship or most of a projector.

For a church under 100 people the comparison starts lower still. Harvest's Small Team plan is **$31.67/mo, billed as $380 every 12 months**, and it carries the things a small church actually opens on a Sunday: Check-In System (QR), Livestream + Live Giving, Church Map, Newsletter, Sermon Notes → Livestream, the donation page, 500 contacts and 5 admin accounts, 5 courses, and Docs & Notes.

The platform fee on every paid Harvest plan is **0%**. That is the platform's own cut, and it is zero on Individual, Small Team and Ministry alike. Card processing is a separate cost charged by the processor, on any platform — Tithely publishes theirs at 2.9% + $0.30 for credit and debit, 1% + $0.30 for ACH ([get.tithe.ly/pricing](https://get.tithe.ly/pricing), read 1 September 2026); Harvest does not publish processing rates on this site, and no church should read "0% platform fee" as "a $100 gift arrives as $100" anywhere.

Three rows there are worth reading slowly, because they are where the bill quietly grows and where the honest answer runs both ways. Courses and livestream are not listed on Tithely's pricing page (read 1 September 2026), which means a church that disciples people through material — a membership class, a new believers' course, a leadership track — goes and buys somewhere to put it, and so does a church that streams. Going the other way, volunteer scheduling and service planning are things Tithely's Church Management carries and Harvest's plans do not list, so a church whose Sunday hinges on a rota is buying that separately from us. None of that is a failing on either side; their pricing page describes a church management system with an app, a site and worship tools around it, and it does that job. It just is not the whole list, and neither is ours.

The rota, though, is one afternoon a month. The member list that has to stay right across four products is every week, forever, and it is the cost nobody puts in the spreadsheet: a family updates their address in the giving tool and it never reaches the database; someone re-enters check-in data by hand because the app and the ChMS disagree about who the kids belong to; an integration breaks on somebody else's release schedule and a volunteer spends a Tuesday evening finding out why. Four subscriptions is four renewal dates, four support queues and four places a name can be wrong.

## The argument, plainly

Harvest's case is not that it does any one thing better than a specialist. It is that a church needs eight or nine things and a specialist sells you one. Every product in the stack above is fine at its job. The problem is the stack — the total on the card, the drift between the lists, the hours that never appear on an invoice.

One subscription covers it. Ministry at $63.34/mo, billed as $760 every 12 months, covers your app on your own domain, the database, giving, check-in, livestream, courses, the community feed, the blog, the newsletter and the end-of-year statements. The platform fee on that is 0%. Small Team at $31.67/mo, billed as $380 every 12 months, covers most of it for a church under 100.
