No church runs on one tool.

It runs on a website, a member database, a giving page, kids' check-in, a community space, courses, a livestream and tax receipts in January. That is the real list, and it is why most churches searching for a Breeze ChMS alternative are not unhappy with Breeze. They are unhappy that the list keeps growing a vendor at a time: one subscription for the database, another for the course library, another for the stream, each with its own bill and its own copy of the congregation. Harvest is built the other way round — one subscription carrying the whole list, with one member record underneath it.

So this is not a feature-by-feature scorecard. It is one platform against an assembled stack, and what each costs a month. Every competitor price below came from that vendor's own pricing page on the date given.

## What Breeze ChMS covers, and what it costs

Breeze ChMS is a church management system, now sold by Tithely as "Church Management + Free Giving (Powered by Breeze ChMS)". Read from [breezechms.com/pricing](https://www.breezechms.com/pricing) on October 20, 2026: that plan is **$72/month**, with no setup fee and no contract. The bundle that adds Breeze Worship Tools, Tithely Church Apps and Tithely Sites is **$119/month**.

Their [support pricing article](https://support.breezechms.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002438453-Pricing-Features-Tech-Requirements), read the same day, lists what the $72 buys: unlimited people, unlimited users, unlimited accounts, online giving, text giving, name-tag printing, event management and registration, forms, email and SMS, year-end giving statements and contribution reports. Label printers and barcode scanners are bought separately.

The pricing page says what it is: church management. A course library a church can hand to a new believer, and a livestream its own members watch inside the church's app, are not on it (read October 20, 2026). A church that wants those buys them somewhere else, and that is where the bill starts moving.

## So here is what a church actually pays

Take the full Tithely bundle at **$119/month** — management, Worship Tools, the app and the site — and add the two rows its pricing page does not cover.

Courses: [thinkific.com/pricing](https://www.thinkific.com/pricing/), read October 20, 2026, starts at **$54/month** billed monthly on Basic, or $40/month billed annually. Livestreaming: [vimeo.com/upgrade-plan](https://vimeo.com/upgrade-plan), read October 20, 2026, lists Advanced at **$75 per seat/month**, and its own FAQ says live streaming needs Advanced or Enterprise.

Add them up. **$248 a month, across three vendors, on three separate bills** — and that prices one Vimeo seat, not two.

Every Harvest figure here and in the table above comes from the [Harvest pricing page](/#pricing), read October 20, 2026.

Harvest's Ministry plan is **$80/mo** on the monthly term — $168 a month under that $248, compared like for like — or **$62.67/mo, billed as $752 every 12 months** on a yearly commitment. One plan, one bill, carrying the member CRM, the blog, the giving page, QR check-in, fifteen courses, livestream with live giving, community groups, event registration, custom branding, the accounting and receipt ledger, and giving statements in January.

A church under a hundred people usually does not need Ministry. Small Team is **$40/mo** monthly, or **$31.67/mo, billed as $380 every 12 months**, and it carries the news and community feed, the member CRM, the giving page, five courses, QR check-in, livestream with live giving, the church map, sermon notes pushed to the livestream, the blog and the mobile app — $40 against the same $248.

## The fee line, stated plainly

Harvest's platform fee is **0% on every paid plan** — zero on Individual, Small Team and Ministry alike.

It can be zero because Harvest does not sit between a church and a gift at all. The church publishes its own PayPal, Cash App, Venmo, Zelle, Revolut or Wise links on one giving page in the app, each link checked against its provider before it goes live, and the money moves from the giver to the account the church already owns. There is nothing for Harvest to route, hold or take a share of. What the church's own payment provider charges is a real cost, and not ours to quote.

That is also the honest limit of the comparison, so here it is in one sentence: **Breeze ChMS processes online and text giving inside the platform, and Harvest does not process a card at all.** That is their row, and it is a real one. A gift recorded by hand in Harvest's CRM does post to the dashboard, the accounting ledger and the giver's own history — but someone records it. Breeze also advertises unlimited people on a flat price, where Harvest's plans carry per-tier caps.

And the stack is still a stack: the courses and the livestream come from two other vendors at $129 a month between them.

## The cost that never appears on an invoice

Three subscriptions is three bills. The more expensive problem is that it is three lists of people.

A family joins the church. Someone adds them to the church management system. Do they appear in the course platform? In the streaming account? Only if a volunteer remembers, every time. Six months in, the records live in one place and the course progress in another, and nobody can answer "who has been in our discipleship track all year and has never once given?" without exporting two files and matching them by hand.

Then there are the parts nobody budgets: the reconciling hours, the integration that breaks because a vendor shipped on a Thursday, the second and third login every leader is issued and revoked, the per-product fees on whatever each tool collects, the January scramble to build [giving statements](/blog/year-end-giving-statements-what-to-include) out of records that were never in one place. The [check-in](/blog/track-sunday-attendance-without-a-sign-in-sheet) that only becomes data if the sheet reaches the right system.

One platform means one member record. Giving history, check-in history, group, course progress and prayer requests are attributes of the same person, because there is only one person for them to be attributes of.

## What we would actually say

Harvest's argument has never been that it out-features anybody. It is that a church's software bill and a church's member list should each be one thing rather than several.

Breeze ChMS runs a congregation's records well, and nothing here has argued otherwise. What this post has argued is that almost no church stops at records. The discipleship track comes, then the stream, then the site — each one a separate decision, a separate invoice and a separate list of names. Harvest is the bet that a church would rather make that decision once, at **$80/mo** for Ministry or **$62.67/mo billed as $752 every 12 months**.

One subscription. One member record. One login. That is the whole of Harvest's argument, and it is the whole of this post.
